# Demand evaluation - 27 October 2026

Implemented and evaluated from the supplied ML_database.sql without executing SQL, changing database records, or modifying the manuscript or EDC model. No database migration is required.

## Conclusion

Daily demand and peak warnings improved, but the selected 30-day-total forecasts still do not outperform the recent-average baseline. Vehicle total-demand R-squared remains negative. These results do not support calling the entire forecasting system passed or reliably accurate.

## Scope and method

- Source: 4,000 synthetic booking requests, reservation dates 2023-10-16 through 2026-10-09; all statuses included.
- Retrospective evaluation: 2026-04-13 through 2026-10-09; six non-overlapping 30-day horizons (180 daily observations, six total observations).
- Each origin selects count models using six earlier 30-day windows, minimizing total MAE, with daily RMSE breaking ties. It then refits using available history. Previous test outcomes are usable only after they become past observations.
- Candidates include historical/weekday baselines, calendar Poisson, Holt-Winters, no-trend seasonal smoothing, an ensemble, and booking pickup models. The chosen method can change across origins; the system is not exclusively Holt-Winters.
- Pickup models use requests already submitted by the cutoff plus an estimate of requests still to arrive, based on historical submission lead times. Future-created bookings are excluded. Reservation-date edits cannot be reconstructed from this snapshot.
- Peak probabilities are selected by earlier Brier score; warning cutoffs maximize earlier F1. The peak count threshold comes from history before those validation windows. Booking-aware Poisson probabilities were selected at all six evaluated origins for both categories.
- Some evaluation periods had already been inspected, and candidates were revised during this investigation. This is a retrospective development comparison, not a new untouched final test or evidence of real-world accuracy.

## Full six-period 30-day-total evaluation

Errors are requests per 30-day period. These totals measure a different quantity from daily errors.

| Category | Model MAE | Recent-mean MAE | Model RMSE | Recent-mean RMSE | Model R2 | Model wMAPE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| vehicle_demand | 12.9406 | 8.6667 | 14.8750 | 10.4060 | -1.5989 | 17.53% |
| facility_demand | 12.8355 | 8.7976 | 15.3188 | 12.2578 | 0.1189 | 18.08% |

Both selected models have larger total-demand errors than the recent-mean baseline on these six periods, despite being chosen using earlier validation. Positive R2 alone would not establish superiority to that practical baseline. The recent-mean forecast is the stronger observed comparator for total-demand planning in this evaluation; no unseen test confirms a deployment choice made after inspecting these results.

## Peak warnings across 180 days

| Category | Precision | Recall | F1 | Brier | Actual peaks | Correct warnings | False alarms | Missed peaks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| vehicle_demand | 79.31% | 63.89% | 0.7077 | 0.0875 | 36 | 23 | 6 | 13 |
| facility_demand | 54.55% | 64.29% | 0.5902 | 0.0818 | 28 | 18 | 15 | 10 |

Higher precision/recall/F1 is better; lower Brier is better. Probabilities and classification performance are not the same as a regression success rate. Warnings describe demand risk, not resource availability or assignment feasibility.

Some peaks are already evident from requests submitted before the forecast date. Counting these is legitimate operationally, but it should not be presented as purely predicting unseen requests:

- vehicle_demand: 11 peak days already evident from known bookings. On the remaining days, precision is 66.67% and recall is 48.00%.
- facility_demand: 6 peak days already evident from known bookings. On the remaining days, precision is 44.44% and recall is 54.55%.
